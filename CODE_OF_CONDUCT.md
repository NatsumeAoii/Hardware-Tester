# Code Of Conduct

## Purpose

This project should be a practical, respectful place to improve a hardware diagnostic tool. The standard is simple: communicate clearly, act in good faith, and keep the work safe for maintainers, contributors, and users.

## Expected Behavior

- Be respectful and direct.
- Discuss technical decisions with evidence and context.
- Give and receive feedback constructively.
- Keep disagreements focused on the work, not the person.
- Respect different experience levels, backgrounds, languages, and time zones.
- Protect user privacy and security when discussing bugs, reports, logs, or device behavior.

## Unacceptable Behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, or personal attacks.
- Discriminatory language or conduct.
- Sexualized comments or unwanted attention.
- Trolling, repeated disruption, or bad-faith arguments.
- Publishing private communication without consent.
- Sharing secrets, credentials, private user data, or unauthorized vulnerability details.
- Encouraging unsafe testing against systems or devices without permission.

## Security And Privacy Expectations

This project touches browser hardware APIs and diagnostic data. Contributors must not request, publish, or retain sensitive device/user data unless it is strictly necessary and explicitly authorized.

When reporting bugs:

- Remove personal information from screenshots, logs, and reports.
- Do not include credentials, tokens, private network details, or precise location data.
- Label security-sensitive reports clearly and send them through a private channel.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take action when behavior harms the project or its participants. Actions may include:

- Clarifying expectations.
- Editing or removing comments.
- Closing issues or pull requests.
- Temporary or permanent restriction from project spaces.

Enforcement should be proportionate, consistent, and focused on protecting the project community.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns to the project maintainer. Include:

- What happened.
- Where it happened.
- Who was involved.
- Any links or context needed to understand the concern.

Maintainers should handle reports with care and avoid unnecessary disclosure.
